Problem 1(a) - Observation on Tokenizer vs Lemmatizer
I implemented two versions as required:

Simple Tokenizer (using regex [a-zA-Z0-9]+ as introduced in Exercise Sheet #1)
NLP Pipeline (using Stanford CoreNLP for tokenization + lemmatization + stopword removal)

Comparison of top-25 terms and top-25 documents under the top-25 latent concepts:
Simple Tokenizer version suffers from heavy noise:

Many Wikipedia template artifacts appear frequently (onlyinclude, notoc, condita, urbe, consulship, domini, anno).
Results are heavily dominated by date/calendar-related terms (julian, calendar, year, october, december, etc.).
Documents are mostly date pages or disambiguation pages, making the latent concepts semantically weak and less meaningful.

NLP (Lemmatizer) version produces significantly better results:

Template noise is effectively removed.
Terms are cleaner and more semantically coherent (e.g., nordic, winter, mythology, norse, autosport, climate, disulfide, bacterial).
Documents are more relevant and diverse (e.g., Fimbulwinter, Geography of Lithuania, Autosport, chemical/biological concepts).
Latent concepts show clearer topical structure (mythology, winter events, chemistry, geography, etc.).

Conclusion:
The NLP pipeline with lemmatization produces much higher quality latent concepts compared to the simple regex tokenizer. 
The difference is very obvious — the simple tokenizer version is dominated by noise and date fragments, while the NLP version yields interpretable and meaningful topics. 
This clearly demonstrates the importance of proper NLP preprocessing (lemmatization + stopword handling) for LSA.
