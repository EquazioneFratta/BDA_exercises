### 3. Hyper-Parameters & Execution Results
* SVD Hyper-parameters: The model was trained using `numFreq = 5000` and `k = 25` latent dimensions.
* Dataset: 134,164 Wikipedia movie articles.
* Total Runtime: Approximately 6.5 minutes on the IRIS cluster utilizing 28 cores.

### 4. SVD Manual Inspection & Analysis
A manual inspection of the top-25 terms and top-25 documents across the 25 latent concepts confirms that the SVD decomposition significantly improves document representation. The concepts successfully cluster the movies into highly coherent semantic macro-themes that are often more descriptive than the original genre labels:
* Concept 9: Effectively groups crime-related terms (murder, detective, police, killer) and almost exclusively retrieves movies originally labeled as [mystery] and [crime].
* Concept 15: Accurately captures academic settings (student, school, college, teacher), returning films that revolve around educational environments.
* Concept 20: Isolates a well-defined cluster of historical, medieval, or monarchic-themed movies (king, prince, princess, kingdom).

### 5. Search Engine Evaluation
The keyword query search engine was tested using 5 distinct queries. While the retrieval system performs well overall at matching semantic concepts, it also highlights an intrinsic limitation of LSA regarding term weighting. For example:
* The query ['alien', 'space', 'ship'] primarily retrieved naval warfare movies (e.g., "Neutral Port" [war], "The Hook" [war]) rather than science fiction films. This occurs because the highly frequent term "ship" mathematically dominates the query vector, outweighing the semantic pull of the word "alien".
* Conversely, the query ['detective', 'murder', 'mystery'] performed exceptionally well, returning highly relevant titles like "The Case of Gabriel Perry" [crime] and "Accused" [drama].
