Conclusion & Hyperparameter Analysis: 
Regarding the binary classification ('HeartDisease'), our grid search 
revealed that shallow Decision Trees (maxDepth=6) generalized best, 
achieving an internal validation AUC-ROC of ~0.61 (using entropy). 
Deeper trees (maxDepth=12 and 24) suffered from severe overfitting, 
causing the validation AUC-ROC to drop to ~0.50 - 0.55. Furthermore, 
'entropy' consistently outperformed 'gini' as an impurity criterion 
across all depths.

Regarding the multiclass target ('AgeCategory'), both models struggle 
significantly, with Random Forest performing only marginally better 
than the GBT (OneVsRest) approach (~17.4% vs ~17.0% overall accuracy). 
The per-class metrics reveal that the models perform slightly better 
on the youngest (18-24) and oldest (80+) brackets, but completely fail 
to classify the middle age groups (e.g., 45-54). Finally, attempting to 
build deep Random Forests (maxDepth=24) with multiple trees generated 
massive broadcast blocks (up to ~300 MB per task), causing severe RAM 
and network congestion on the cluster.
